Harmony Exercise 4b

Exercise

These are exercises in four-part harmony (soprano, alto, tenor and bass) in the style taught in Spanish conservatories. In a connection that keeps the common tone, any note that two consecutive chords share stays in the same voice, while the other voices move to the nearest chord tone. This gives smooth voice leading and avoids parallel fifths and octaves.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a chord connection that keeps the common tone?
It means holding in the same voice the note (or notes) that two consecutive chords have in common, while the other voices move as little as possible.
Which voice should hold the common tone?
The voice that already has it in the first chord. If the soprano has G in the first chord and G also belongs to the second chord, the soprano holds that G.
When can you keep a common tone between two chords?
When the two chords share at least one note. In C major, I and IV share C, and I and V share G. Chords with nothing in common, such as IV and V, cannot be connected this way.
What does it mean to name the key of a connection?
It means finding the major or minor key in which both chords make sense, by working out the functions (tonic, subdominant, dominant) they could have there. A single pair of chords often fits more than one key.
What mistakes should you avoid?
Moving the common tone instead of holding it, writing parallel fifths or octaves in the other voices, not resolving the leading tone, and crossing voices without a reason.
