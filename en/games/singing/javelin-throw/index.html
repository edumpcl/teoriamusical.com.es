Sing a glissando between two notes and throw the javelin as far as you can. The game gives you an interval: you hold the first note (the run-up), slide your voice to the second (the throw) and hold it (the finish). A lane shows you where your voice should go, and the longer you stay inside it, the farther the javelin flies. There is nothing to install: it works in the browser on your phone or computer.

When you press “Let’s throw!”, your browser will ask for permission to use the microphone. The audio is not recorded or sent anywhere: it is analyzed on your own device.

How to play

  1. Choose a musician, type your name and pick the difficulty and the intervals you want to practice.
  2. Listen to the example. You hear the starting note on the piano, then a glissando that slides up or down to the arrival note, and finally the arrival note on the piano. You also see the lane drawn on screen.
  3. Run-up (1 second): sing the first note and hold it.
  4. Throw (2 seconds): slide your voice, without leaps and at a constant speed, to the second note.
  5. Finish (1 second): hold the second note until the end. The javelin takes off.

The interval can go up or down, and the game tells you which. You can sing in whichever octave is comfortable: the lane moves relative to the note you sing, not to a fixed pitch.

The lane and the scoring

The lane is the ideal path for your voice: a flat line during the run-up, a straight ramp during the glissando and another flat line in the finish. Your voice is drawn on top of it: green when you are inside and orange when you drift out. Every second inside the lane adds meters, and it counts for more as the throw goes on:

PhaseLengthMeters per second insideMaximum
Run-up1 s20 m20 m
Glissando2 s25 m50 m
Finish1 s30 m30 m

With a perfect 4 seconds you throw 100 m, and the javelin world record is 98.48 m. If you are inside the lane for less than 0.6 seconds in total, it is a no throw.

The three difficulties

The difficulty changes the help you get and the width of the lane, measured in cents (a half step (UK: semitone) is 100 cents):

DifficultyLane widthHelp
Easy±50 centsYou see the note names (for example, C → E) and hear the whole example.
Normal±30 centsYou see the name of the interval and whether it goes up or down, and hear the whole example.
Master±15 centsOnly the first note plays; you have to do the glissando by ear.

The intervals

You choose which intervals can come up. The first note is C, D, E, F, G or A, and the glissando can go up or down:

What is a glissando?

A glissando is a continuous slide from one note to another, passing through all the pitches in between instead of jumping from one to the other. The voice does it naturally, and so do instruments without fixed pitches, such as the violin or the slide trombone. It is hard to sing well because you have to control the pitch at every instant, not just at the two ends: the ramp of the lane is a straight line in cents, that is, a slide at a constant speed.

If you would rather practice a single note, start with the long jump; if you want to sing two separate notes, try the high jump; and for a whole chord, the triple jump.

Tips for the glissando

  • Start sliding right at second 1 and reach the second note at 3 seconds. Watch the cursor on the graph.
  • Slide at a constant speed. Large intervals need more air and a faster slide; do not slow down at the start or rush at the end.
  • Sing “ah”, “oh” or “oo” instead of consonants: the microphone detects a long vowel better.
  • Find a quiet place. A TV or several voices at once confuse the pitch detector.
  • Start with Easy and the 2nds and 3rds and move up when you have mastered them.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the lane in the game?
It is the ideal path your voice should follow: flat during the run-up (first note), a straight ramp during the glissando and flat during the finish (second note). Every second you stay inside adds meters to the throw.
How many meters does each phase add?
Each second inside the lane adds 20 m in the run-up, 25 m in the glissando and 30 m in the finish. With a perfect 4 seconds you throw 100 m; the world record is 98.48 m.
What changes between Easy, Normal and Master?
The width of the lane (±50, ±30 or ±15 cents) and the help: in Easy you see the note names, in Normal the name of the interval and whether it goes up or down, and in Master only the first note plays.
Do I have to sing in the same octave I hear?
No. The lane moves relative to the note you sing, so you can use whichever octave is comfortable.
What is a glissando?
A continuous slide from one note to another, passing through all the pitches in between instead of jumping from one to the other. The voice, the violin or the slide trombone can do it.
