Build the Natural Minor Scale

The name of a natural minor scale appears. Your task is to draw its 8 notes on the staff. The natural minor scale (the Aeolian mode) is the "pure" minor scale, with no raised leading tone: it is what you get by playing the notes of its relative major starting from the 6th degree.

How to use the exercise: Click (or drag) on the staff to place each note. First choose the accidental with the ♭ ♮ ♯ buttons if the note needs one. Undo removes the last note and Clear restarts the exercise. The notes are sorted automatically from low to high. The magnifier shows the note you are about to place.

The pattern of the natural minor scale

The pattern is W – H – W – W – H – W – W (whole step, half step, whole step, whole step, half step, whole step, whole step; UK: tone and semitone). The half step between the 2nd and the 3rd degree gives the scale its minor 3rd and its character, in contrast to the major 3rd of the major scale.

The natural minor and its relative major

Each natural minor scale shares its key signature with a major scale: they are relative keys. The minor tonic is a minor third below the major tonic (or the 6th degree of the major scale). That is why A minor uses only the white keys, just like C major, but with A as the tonal center.

To lock these scales into your fingers, practice them with the online metronome at 60-70% of your target tempo, raising it by 5 BPM each time you play three repetitions without mistakes. The online tuner lets you check your tuning before you start.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the natural minor scale?
It is the minor scale that uses the key signature of its relative major, with no accidentals added. It is also called the Aeolian mode. Its pattern is W-H-W-W-H-W-W. It has a minor 3rd, a minor 6th and a minor 7th.
How is it related to the major scale?
Every natural minor scale is the relative of a major scale: they share the same key signature. The minor scale starts on the 6th degree of its relative major. A minor and C major, for example, use the same notes.
Why is it called "natural"?
Because it does not raise the 7th degree, as the harmonic and melodic minor scales do. It keeps the degrees given by the key signature of the minor key.
Does the natural minor scale have a leading tone?
Not in the strict sense. Its 7th degree is a whole step below the tonic (a subtonic), not a half step. To get a true leading tone you have to raise the 7th degree, which gives the harmonic minor scale.
How do I build it step by step?
Place the tonic and apply the pattern W-H-W-W-H-W-W going up, using the accidentals of the matching minor key signature. Each of the 7 letter names appears exactly once.
