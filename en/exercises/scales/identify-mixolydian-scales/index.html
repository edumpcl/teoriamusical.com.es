Identify Mixolydian Scales

The staff shows the 8 notes of a Mixolydian scale, and your task is to recognize which one it is. On easy you choose from 4 options; on hard you first pick the root note and then its accidental.

Tip: Mixolydian is a major-sounding mode with a minor 7th: the 7th degree is lowered by a half step compared with the major scale.

What is the Mixolydian scale?

The Mixolydian scale is the major scale with the 7th degree lowered by a half step. Its pattern of whole steps (UK: tones) and half steps (UK: semitones) is W-W-H-W-W-H-W. It sounds major but has no leading tone, which gives it an open character typical of blues, rock and folk music.

Example: G Mixolydian

Start from G major (G-A-B-C-D-E-F♯) and lower the 7th degree (F♯ becomes F♮): G-A-B-C-D-E-F-G. The key signature is the one of G major (one sharp), and the 7th is lowered with a natural sign.

The key signature of Mixolydian

A Mixolydian scale uses the key signature of the major scale on the same tonic. The lowered 7th is not in the key signature: it is written as an accidental, a natural sign if the key signature raised that note, or a flat if it did not alter it.

To lock these scales into your fingers, practice them with the online metronome at 60-70% of your target tempo. The online tuner lets you check your tuning first. You can also build it note by note.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Mixolydian scale?
It is a major mode: the major scale with the 7th degree lowered by a half step. It keeps the major 3rd but has a minor 7th, so there is no leading tone.
How is it different from the major scale?
Only in the 7th degree: Mixolydian lowers it by a half step. The other notes are the same as in the major scale.
What key signature does the Mixolydian scale have?
The key signature of the major scale on the same tonic. The lowered 7th is not in the key signature; it is written as an accidental.
Where is the Mixolydian scale used?
In blues, rock, funk and many folk traditions, because of its minor 7th. It is also the scale that fits a dominant seventh chord.
