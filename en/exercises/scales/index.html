Scale Exercises

Custom scale worksheets to print

Besides the quizzes, you can create your own printable worksheet here. You choose which scales go in (all the major ones, all the minor ones, both, or only the types you want), whether the task is to identify them, write them with accidentals or write them with a key signature, the clef, how many accidentals the key signature has and how many scales. A new sheet comes out each time, with its answer key, ready to print.

Each scale has its own staff, and the melodic minor is written complete, going up and down. Every sheet has a number: below the buttons a link appears that brings back exactly the same sheet, to reprint it or to give the same one to a whole class. You can photocopy them freely for the classroom.

If you prefer ready-made sheets of a single type, each exercise has its own PDF with answers. For example: identify harmonic minor scales, write major scales or write the Dorian scale with a key signature.

How to practice scales

The recommended order is to start with the major scales, moving gradually around the circle of fifths. Next come the natural minor scales (the same key signatures as their relative majors), then the harmonic minor scales (with the raised 7th and its characteristic augmented second) and finally the melodic minor scales.

In the identify exercises the scales appear both with a key signature and with the accidentals written note by note, so you practice both ways of reading found in real scores.

To lock these scales into your fingers, practice them with the online metronome at 60-70% of your target tempo, raising it by 5 BPM each time you play three repetitions without mistakes. The online tuner lets you check your tuning before you start.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many types of scales are practiced here?
Six main types: major, harmonic major (6th lowered), melodic major (6th and 7th lowered), natural minor, harmonic minor (7th raised) and melodic minor (6th and 7th raised going up). Each type has 15 keys. The harmonic and melodic major scales are called "mixed" major scales (mixta principal and mixta secundaria) in Spanish-language conservatories. Two modes, Mixolydian and Dorian, and the chromatic scale are also included.
What is the difference between the harmonic and the melodic minor scale?
The harmonic minor scale raises the 7th degree compared with the natural minor, creating an augmented second between the 6th and the 7th that gives it its characteristic sound. The melodic minor scale raises the 6th degree too, going up, to avoid that augmented second, which gives a smoother, more vocal line.
How do I identify a scale on the staff?
Use the key signature to find the tonic, then look at the intervals between consecutive degrees. The pattern W-W-H-W-W-W-H (whole and half steps) means a major scale. An augmented second between consecutive degrees points to the harmonic minor scale or the harmonic major scale.
How many scales are there in these exercises?
90 scales in the mixed exercise: 15 major, 15 harmonic major, 15 melodic major, 15 natural minor, 15 harmonic minor and 15 melodic minor. The "All Scales" exercise mixes them at random.
In what order should I practice the scales?
Start with the major scales (from C major around the circle of fifths), then the harmonic and melodic major scales, then the natural minor scales (which share key signatures with their relative majors), the harmonic and finally the melodic minor. That order keeps the number of new accidentals small at each step.
