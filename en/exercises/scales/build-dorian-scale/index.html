Build the Dorian Scale

The name of a Dorian scale appears. Your task is to draw its 8 notes on the staff. The Dorian scale is the natural minor scale with the 6th raised by a half step: a "brighter" minor, symmetrical, widely used in jazz, folk and modal music.

How to use the exercise: Click (or drag) on the staff to place each note. First choose the accidental with the ♭ ♮ ♯ buttons if the note needs one. Undo removes the last note and Clear restarts the exercise. The notes are sorted automatically from low to high. The magnifier shows the note you are about to place.

The pattern of the Dorian scale

The pattern is W – H – W – W – W – H – W (UK: tone and semitone). It is symmetrical (it reads the same from the top down) and differs from the natural minor only in its raised 6th, which gives it a more open color.

The raised 6th, the mark of the Dorian mode

Compared with the natural minor, the Dorian scale raises the 6th by a half step. That makes it the second mode of the major scale: D Dorian uses the white keys (the notes of C major starting on D). Its minor 3rd with a major 6th gives it a character that is both melancholy and bright.

To lock these scales into your fingers, practice them with the online metronome at 60-70% of your target tempo, raising it by 5 BPM each time you play three repetitions without mistakes. The online tuner lets you check your tuning before you start.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Dorian scale?
It is the second mode of the major scale: a natural minor scale with the 6th raised by a half step. Its pattern is W-H-W-W-W-H-W and it is symmetrical. It has a minor 3rd but a major 6th.
How is it different from the natural minor scale?
Only in the 6th, which is raised by a half step in Dorian. That single change gives it a brighter, more open color than the natural minor.
Why does D Dorian have no accidentals?
Because the Dorian mode is built on the 2nd degree of the major scale, and D is the 2nd degree of C major. D Dorian uses the white keys: D-E-F-G-A-B-C-D, with B natural as the raised 6th.
What is the Dorian scale used for?
It is common in modal jazz, rock, Celtic folk and medieval music. It works well over minor seventh chords (m7) and gives a minor sound that is not dark.
How do I build it step by step?
Start from the natural minor scale on the tonic and raise only the 6th degree by a half step, keeping the letter name of that note. All the other degrees stay the same.
