Build the Harmonic Minor Scale with Key Signature

The way it is done in class: first write the key signature of the key, then the harmonic minor scale. The notes inherit the key signature, so you only mark the raised 7th, the leading tone, with a natural (or sharp), because it is not part of the key signature.

How to use the exercise: Step 1 (key signature): choose Sharp or Flat and click on the staff to place each accidental of the key signature in its order. Step 2 (scale): with the key signature in place, put in the notes of the scale. They inherit the key signature automatically; press ♮ / ♯ / ♭ only when a note needs an accidental of its own. Undo and Clear work in each step. The magnifier shows what you are about to place.

The leading tone is not in the key signature

The harmonic minor scale is the natural minor scale with the 7th raised to create a leading tone. That raise is not part of the key signature: it is written as an accidental on the note. So you first place the key signature of the minor key and then raise the 7th degree with a sharp or, if the key signature already lowered it, with a natural sign.

Sharp or natural, depending on the key signature

In A harmonic minor the 7th degree (G) is raised with a sharp (G♯), because the key signature does not alter it. In C harmonic minor the key signature includes B♭, so to raise the 7th (B) you use a natural sign (B♮). The letter name of the note never changes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is the raised 7th not in the key signature?
Because the key signature belongs to the natural minor scale. The leading tone is an accidental of the harmonic minor scale, so it is written note by note, not in the key signature.
When do I use a natural sign for the leading tone?
When the key signature already lowers that note. In C minor (B♭ in the key signature), raising the 7th (B) needs a natural sign: B♮.
Where is the augmented second?
Between the 6th degree (unaltered) and the raised 7th. In A harmonic minor, between F and G♯.
Does the note name change when I raise it?
No. It is still the 7th degree; it only goes up a half step, with a sharp or a natural sign.
