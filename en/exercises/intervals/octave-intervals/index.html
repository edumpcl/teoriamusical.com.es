Intervals of an Octave

The octave is the most consonant interval. It doubles the frequency, and it is the foundation of the Western musical system: two notes an octave apart share the same name and are heard as the same note in different registers.

1. Visual shortcut: spotting a octave on the staff

Octaves are a matter of recognizing spatial patterns on the staff.

The mirror rule: an octave always changes the kind of position, like fourths and sixths.

  • If the lower note is on a line, the upper note is in a space.
  • If the lower note is in a space, the upper note is on a line.

An octave covers exactly 4 lines and 4 spaces, with six free positions between the notes. It is a wide, balanced distance. Example in treble clef: C4 (first ledger line below) and C5 (third space).

2. Reference table

The octave is usually perfect, but in advanced theory its variants are also considered:

Interval quality Half steps Harmonic classification
Diminished 8th (d8) 11 Conditional dissonance (enharmonic to a major 7th)
Perfect octave (P8) 12 Perfect consonance
Augmented 8th (A8) 13 Conditional dissonance (enharmonic to a minor 9th)

Frequently Asked Questions

How many half steps does a perfect octave have?
Exactly 12 half steps (UK: semitones). It is the interval of repetition: the same note in a different register. Two notes with the same name, 12 half steps apart, form a perfect octave.
How do you recognize an octave on the staff?
An octave covers 8 positions. Both notes have the same name, but they sit on different kinds of position (one on a line and the other in a space, or the other way around), with six positions between them. In treble clef, C4 (first ledger line below the staff) and C5 (third space) are an octave apart.
Why does the octave sound so consonant?
Because the two notes have a frequency ratio of 2:1, the simplest ratio after the unison. The octave is also the first overtone of every note: when you play C3, the strongest extra pitch it produces is C4.
What does the 8va sign mean?
The 8va sign above a note or passage means that it is played one octave higher than written. The 8vb sign (ottava bassa) below means one octave lower. Both signs avoid too many ledger lines.
What types of octaves exist?
The perfect octave (12 half steps) is the common one. The augmented octave (13 half steps, enharmonic to a minor 9th) and the diminished octave (11 half steps, enharmonic to a major 7th) are possible in theory but very rare. When an "octave" appears in analysis, it is almost always perfect.
