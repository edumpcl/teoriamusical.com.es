Intervals of a Fourth

The fourth is a basic distance in harmony. It is the interval between the tonic and the subdominant of a key, and it appears everywhere in classical music, jazz and folk music.

1. Visual shortcut: spotting a fourth on the staff

The crossing rule: a fourth always changes the kind of position.

  • If the lower note is on a line, the upper note is in a space.
  • If the lower note is in a space, the upper note is on a line.

Between the two notes there are two free positions (a line and a space, or a space and a line). Example in treble clef: E4 (first line) and A4 (second space), with F4 and G4 skipped.

2. Reference table

The perfect 4th is one of the most stable and powerful intervals in music. Here are the three types:

Interval quality Half steps Harmonic classification
Diminished 4th (d4) 4 Conditional dissonance (enharmonic to a major 3rd)
Perfect 4th (P4) 5 Perfect consonance
Augmented 4th (A4) 6 Semi-consonance (tritone)

Frequently Asked Questions

How many half steps does a perfect 4th have?
A perfect 4th has 5 half steps (UK: semitones). In a major key it appears between degrees 1–4, 2–5, 3–6, 5–1 (upward), 6–2 and 7–3. The augmented 4th (tritone) has 6 half steps and the diminished 4th has 4.
How do you recognize a fourth on the staff?
A fourth covers 4 positions of the staff: one note on a line and the other in a space (or the other way around). There are two positions between the notes, one line and one space.
What is the tritone?
The tritone is the augmented 4th (or diminished 5th): 6 half steps, exactly half of an octave. In C major it occurs between F and B (an augmented 4th). It is a very tense interval and asks for resolution.
Is a fourth consonant or dissonant?
It depends on the context. On its own, and in melodies, the perfect 4th sounds stable, and these exercises classify it as a perfect consonance. But in strict counterpoint, a perfect 4th between the bass and an upper voice is treated as a dissonance that needs to resolve.
How many perfect fourths are in the C major scale?
Six: C–F, D–G, E–A, G–C, A–D and B–E. The only fourth that is not perfect is F–B, an augmented 4th (tritone) with 6 half steps instead of 5.
