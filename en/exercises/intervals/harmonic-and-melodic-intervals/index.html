Harmonic and Melodic Intervals

How the two notes are placed in time decides whether an interval is harmonic (together) or melodic (in sequence).

Harmonic interval and melodic interval

Harmonic interval

The two notes sound at the same time: they are stacked on the staff at the same horizontal position. This is the basic unit of harmony.

Melodic interval

The notes sound one after the other: you read them from left to right. This is the basic unit of melody. A melodic interval can be ascending or descending.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a harmonic and a melodic interval?
In a harmonic interval, the two notes sound together (stacked vertically on the staff) and form harmony. In a melodic interval, the notes come in sequence (horizontally) and form a melody. The number and quality of the interval are the same in both cases; only the way it is presented changes.
How do you spot harmonic intervals on the staff?
Harmonic intervals look like two notes stacked at the same horizontal position, one above the other. You analyze them by counting from the lower note to the upper note, exactly as you do for melodic intervals.
What are ascending and descending melodic intervals?
An ascending melodic interval goes from a lower note to a higher one (the second note is higher on the staff). A descending interval goes the other way: from a higher note to a lower one. The number and quality are always measured between the lower and the higher note, no matter which one sounds first.
Can a harmonic interval be turned into a melodic one?
Yes. The notes are the same; only the timing changes (together or apart). In analysis it is common to "unfold" a harmonic interval to see it as a melodic one and understand its role in the melody.
Why does it matter to tell them apart?
Harmonic intervals decide the consonance or dissonance of the vertical sound, while melodic intervals shape the contour and expression of a melody. In counterpoint, for example, you study which melodic intervals work best in each voice and which harmonic intervals create tension or rest.
