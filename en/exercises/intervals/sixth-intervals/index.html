Intervals of a Sixth

The sixth is one of the most expressive and melodic intervals. This page covers how to spot it on the staff and how it is classified harmonically.

1. Visual shortcut: spotting a sixth on the staff

The crossing rule: a sixth always changes the kind of position.

  • If the lower note is on a line, the upper note is in a space.
  • If the lower note is in a space, the upper note is on a line.

A sixth covers 6 positions: 3 lines and 3 spaces, with four free positions between the notes. Example in treble clef: E4 (first line) and C5 (third space).

2. Reference table

Sixths are the inversions of thirds. Here are the exact half-step counts (UK: semitones) and the harmonic character of each:

Interval quality Half steps Harmonic classification
Diminished 6th (d6) 7 Conditional dissonance (enharmonic to a perfect 5th)
Minor 6th (m6) 8 Imperfect consonance
Major 6th (M6) 9 Imperfect consonance
Augmented 6th (A6) 10 Conditional dissonance (enharmonic to a minor 7th)

Frequently Asked Questions

How many half steps does a sixth have?
The major 6th has 9 half steps (UK: semitones) and the minor 6th has 8. The augmented 6th (the one used in augmented-sixth chords) has 10. Major and minor sixths are consonant and are often used to harmonize a melody in two voices.
How are thirds and sixths related?
They are inversions of each other: a major third turned upside down becomes a minor sixth, and the other way around. If C–E is a major 3rd (4 half steps), then E–C (the inversion) is a minor 6th (8 half steps). A third and its inversion, a sixth, always add up to 9 in number.
How do you recognize a sixth on the staff?
A sixth covers 6 positions: the two notes are of different kinds (line to space or space to line), with four positions between them.
What role do sixths play in counterpoint and harmonization?
In two-voice counterpoint, thirds and sixths are the preferred intervals for parallel motion, because they are consonant and blend well. Harmonizing a melody in parallel sixths is a classic technique that gives a full, warm sound.
Which sixths in the C major scale are major and which are minor?
Major sixths: C–A, D–B, F–D and G–E. Minor sixths: E–C, A–F and B–G. A memory aid: a sixth is the inversion of a third, so where a third is major, its inverted sixth is minor.
