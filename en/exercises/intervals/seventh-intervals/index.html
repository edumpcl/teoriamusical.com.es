Intervals of a Seventh

The seventh is the main source of tension in Western music. Its need to resolve toward the octave or the sixth drives the harmonic motion in cadences.

1. Visual shortcut: spotting a seventh on the staff

Sevenths need a little more visual practice because the notes are far apart, but there is a reliable shortcut.

The same-kind rule: in a seventh, both notes are of the same kind, like thirds and fifths.

  • If the lower note is on a line, the upper note is on a line (far above).
  • If the lower note is in a space, the upper note is in a space.

Shortcut: picture the octave and take one step away. If it looks like an octave but one note is one position closer, it is a seventh. Example in treble clef: E4 (first line) and D5 (fourth line).

2. Reference table

The tension of a seventh depends on its size: the minor 7th is a mild dissonance and the major 7th is a sharp one.

Interval quality Half steps Harmonic classification
Diminished 7th (d7) 9 Conditional dissonance (enharmonic to a major 6th)
Minor 7th (m7) 10 Absolute dissonance
Major 7th (M7) 11 Absolute dissonance
Augmented 7th (A7) 12 Conditional dissonance (enharmonic to a perfect octave)

3. Identify sevenths

The goal is to identify sevenths at a glance. Accidentals should not confuse you: count the half steps (UK: semitones) from the letter names.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many half steps does a seventh have?
The major 7th has 11 half steps (UK: semitones) and the minor 7th has 10. The diminished 7th has 9. Sevenths are dissonant intervals that ask to resolve to a sixth or to the octave.
Why are sevenths dissonant?
They sit very close to the octave (a consonance) without reaching it, and that creates a strong tension that looks for resolution. The minor 7th is the most common dissonance in dominant seventh chords (such as G–F in C major), which resolve to the tonic chord.
What is the difference between the identify and build exercises?
In the identify exercises, an interval is shown on the staff and you recognize its quality (major, minor, diminished). In the build exercises, you get a root note and place the note that forms the requested seventh, adding the accidental it needs. One trains visual recognition and the other trains construction.
How are seconds and sevenths related?
They are inversions of each other. A major 2nd inverted is a minor 7th (2 + 10 = 12 half steps). A minor 2nd inverted is a major 7th (1 + 11 = 12). Complementary intervals always add up to 9 in number and 12 in half steps.
How do you recognize a seventh on the staff?
A seventh covers 7 positions. Both notes are of the same kind (both on lines or both in spaces), with five positions between them. A second is also a pair of notes on neighboring positions, but in a second the notes are right next to each other, and in a seventh they are almost an octave apart.
