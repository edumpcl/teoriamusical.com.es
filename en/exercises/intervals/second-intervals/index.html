Intervals of a Second

The second is the smallest distance between two different scale degrees. Melodies and scales are built from seconds, so recognizing them instantly is the first skill to train in interval reading.

1. Visual shortcut: spotting a second on the staff

The adjacency rule: the two notes of a second always sit on neighboring positions of the staff, so one note is on a line and the other is in the space right above or below it.

  • Example in treble clef: E4 (first line) and F4 (first space).
  • The two noteheads touch each other, or one is shifted slightly sideways so they do not overlap. There is never a gap between them.

2. Reference table

Here are the exact half-step counts (UK: semitones) and the harmonic classification of every second:

Interval quality Half steps Harmonic classification
Diminished 2nd (d2) 0 Conditional dissonance (enharmonic to a perfect unison)
Minor 2nd (m2) 1 Absolute dissonance
Major 2nd (M2) 2 Absolute dissonance
Augmented 2nd (A2) 3 Conditional dissonance (enharmonic to a minor 3rd)

These exercises train you to identify major and minor seconds. Accidentals (♯ and ♭) matter: always use them to count the half steps correctly.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many types of seconds are there?
There are two main types: the major 2nd (2 half steps, such as C–D, D–E, F–G, G–A and A–B) and the minor 2nd (1 half step, such as E–F and B–C). The augmented 2nd (3 half steps) also exists. It is typical of the harmonic minor scale and some other modes.
How do you recognize a second on the staff?
A second covers two neighboring positions of the staff: one note on a line and the other in the adjacent space, or the other way around. It is the smallest step in the unaltered diatonic system.
Which minor seconds are in the C major scale?
The C major scale has two minor seconds: E–F (scale degrees 3 and 4) and B–C (degrees 7 and 8). The other neighboring pairs (C–D, D–E, F–G, G–A, A–B) are major seconds.
What is an augmented second?
An augmented 2nd is 3 half steps between two notes with different letter names. It appears between the 6th and 7th degrees of the harmonic minor scale: in A harmonic minor, it is F–G♯. It has a distinctive, almost exotic sound.
Why are seconds called stepwise (conjunct) motion?
Because they connect neighboring positions of the staff, moving step by step without skipping. Stepwise motion is the most natural for the human voice and the most common in melodies worldwide. Scales are sequences of major and minor seconds.
