What Note Is It? Soprano Clef

Here the notes are written in the C clef on the 1st line, known as the soprano clef. Name each note you see. The quiz has 10 questions and shows your score at the end.

Tip: The center of the C clef marks middle C, which is the bottom line here. From the bottom, the lines are C E G B D and the spaces are D F A C. Count upward from the first line.

How the soprano clef works

All C clefs work in the same way: the point where the two curves meet marks middle C (C4). In the soprano clef that is the first line, so the whole staff lies above middle C, from C4 up to D5 on the top line. Notes look like treble-clef notes shifted down a third: the bottom line is E4 in treble clef and C4 here.

Where do you find the soprano clef?

It was used for soprano parts in choral music from the 16th to the 18th century, and you still find it in editions that keep the original clefs. Today most soprano parts are printed in treble clef.

To review the theory behind this clef, see the C clef on the 1st line (soprano clef) guide in the dictionary. The online metronome is handy for practicing sight-reading in time.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which line is middle C on in the soprano clef?
The first (bottom) line. Every other line and space is higher.
Why are there so many C clefs?
A C clef can be placed on any of the lines, and its position decides which range fits the staff without ledger lines. The soprano clef (1st line) suits high voices, while the tenor clef (4th line) suits the high register of low instruments.
