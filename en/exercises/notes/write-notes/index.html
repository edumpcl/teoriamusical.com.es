Write Notes on the Staff

Writing a note is the next step after reading it. In these exercises you are given a note name (C, D, E…) and you place the note on the staff in the clef you choose, or in all of them. Click or tap the staff to place the note; any octave is accepted. Each quiz has 10 questions. If you are used to Do Re Mi, remember that C is Do in fixed-do countries.

Treble Clef
Place notes in the treble clef: you get a letter and click the right spot on the staff.
F Clef on the 3rd Line
Place notes in the F clef on the 3rd line: you get a letter and mark its place on the staff.
Bass Clef
Place notes in the bass clef: you get a letter and click its position on the staff.
C Clef on the 1st Line
Place notes in the C clef on the 1st line (soprano clef): you get a letter and click its position.
C Clef on the 2nd Line
Place notes in the C clef on the 2nd line (mezzo-soprano clef) on the staff.
Alto Clef
Place notes in the alto clef: you get a letter and click the right place on the staff.
Tenor Clef
Place notes in the tenor clef: you get a letter and click its position on the staff.
All Clefs
Place notes in a different clef each time, from treble to the C clefs.

If you find writing hard, begin with the note reading exercises, which use the same clefs, or review the musical clefs in the dictionary.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I place a note on the staff?
Click or tap the staff at the position where the note belongs, then press Check. Lines and spaces are both valid positions. Use the anchor note of the clef to count to the right place.
Why is any octave accepted?
The exercise checks the letter name only. A given letter can be written in several places on the staff, and each one is a different octave of the same note.
Are sharps and flats included?
No. These exercises use natural notes only, so you can focus on learning the positions in each clef.
