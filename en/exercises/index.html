Music Exercises

Interactive music theory exercises arranged from basic to advanced. Start with notes and rhythm, then move on to scales, keys and chords. Every quiz uses a real staff and checks your answers right away. Several sections also have printable worksheets with answer keys.

Read and Write Notes

Name the notes on the staff in the treble, bass, alto and tenor clefs, and place a named note on the staff. Ten questions per quiz.

Go to Note Exercises

Note Names: Letters, Solfège, German

Do you know the note names in letters (C D E), in fixed-do solfège (Do Re Mi) and in German, where B is H? Test yourself with 10 quick questions.

Take the Note Names Quiz

Time Signatures

Analyze a time signature, complete measures that are missing a note or rest, recognize the time signature from a written rhythm and find syncopation.

Go to Time Signatures

Rhythm

Note values and rests, ties and dots, syncopation, off-beat notes, tuplets, how a phrase starts and ends, and rhythm dictation, from whole notes to sixteenth notes.

Go to Rhythm Exercises

Ear Training

Listen to a piano melody and decide whether it is major or minor, find the time signature, or name the key. You see it written once you answer.

Go to Ear Training

Tempo and Agogics

What each Italian tempo term means, from Grave to Prestissimo, and the tempo changes: accelerando, ritenuto, a tempo and rubato.

Go to Tempo Exercises

Dynamics

What each marking means, from ppp to fff, and the changes of intensity such as crescendo, sforzando and fortepiano. Order markings from soft to loud.

Go to Dynamics Exercises

Articulation

Legato, staccato, accent, tenuto and marcato: the name and the symbol on the staff, in both directions.

Go to Articulation Exercises

Cadences

Recognize whether a phrase ends with an authentic (perfect or imperfect), plagal or deceptive cadence, or a half cadence, from the last two chords written for piano.

Go to Cadence Exercises

Tonal Functions

Tell whether a chord is tonic, subdominant or dominant in a major key from its written notes. Three levels, with substitutes, inversions and the dominant seventh.

Go to Tonal Functions

Intervals

The foundation of harmony. Interval number, harmonic and melodic intervals, ascending and descending, steps and leaps, consonance and dissonance, half steps and enharmonics; practice from 2nds to octaves, a custom quiz, a complete analysis and printable worksheets.

Go to Interval Exercises

Scales

Identify and build scales on the staff, with a key signature or with written accidentals: major (natural and mixed), Mixolydian, minor (natural, harmonic and melodic), Dorian and chromatic. With a full quiz and custom worksheets.

Go to Scale Exercises

Frequently Asked Questions

What level are the music theory exercises for?
They run from beginner to advanced. Reading and writing notes, and the time signature exercises, are a good starting point for beginners. The exercises on scales, keys and cadences suit students who already read music fluently.
How many exercises are there?
There are interactive quizzes in many sections: notes, note names, time signatures, ear training, tempo, dynamics, articulation, cadences, tonal functions, intervals, scales, keys and chords. Several include printable worksheets with answers. All are free and run in the browser, with nothing to install.
In what order should I do the exercises?
From the basic to the advanced: first reading and writing notes, then time signatures and rhythm, then scales and keys, and finally chords, cadences and tonal functions. Each block builds on the one before it. You cannot build scales or chords without reading notes first.
Do the exercises use a real staff?
Yes. The notes appear on a staff drawn in the browser, with the clefs and accidentals written as in printed music, so you practice reading real notation.
Do I need to register?
No. The exercises are free and do not need an account.
