What Harmonic Function Does This Chord Have?

In this exercise you see one chord written in a major key, with its key signature, and you must say which harmonic function it has: tonic, subdominant or dominant. Three levels, instant feedback and an explanation of each chord.

A tip to start: look in the chord for the leading tone (the note a half step below the tonic; UK: leading note). If the chord has it, it is a dominant.

How to recognize the function of a chord

For the chords in this exercise you can recognize the function with a practical three-step rule: look first for the leading tone and, if it is not there, check whether the 4th degree of the scale is present. This is a simplification designed for these chords in a major key, not a method for every chord in tonal harmony.

  1. Does it have the leading tone (the 7th degree of the scale)? Then it is a dominant. This is the case for V, V7 and vii°.
  2. If not, does it have the 4th degree? Then it is a subdominant: IV (4-6-1) and ii (2-4-6).
  3. If it has neither, it is a tonic: I (1-3-5) and its substitute vi (6-1-3).

For example, in C major the leading tone is B and the 4th degree is F: G–B–D has the B (dominant); D–F–A has the F and not the B (subdominant); A–C–E has neither (tonic).

Inversions do not change the function

An inverted chord keeps the same function: it depends on the scale degree the chord is built on, not on the note in the bass. At level 3 you will see triads in root position, first inversion and second inversion, and the V7 in root position, first inversion and second inversion.

Why does the iii chord not appear?

Because, depending on the context, the iii chord acts as a tonic or as a dominant. An exercise with two possible answers would not be fair, so it is not asked. The full theory is on the page about harmonic functions.

Before this exercise

It helps to know the degrees of the scale and how to build triads. After this exercise, practice with the cadence exercises, which combine these functions two at a time.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you know if a chord is tonic, subdominant or dominant?
Look at its notes in the key: if it has the leading tone (7th degree) it is a dominant; if it does not but has the 4th degree it is a subdominant; and if it has neither it is a tonic.
Which chords have a tonic function?
The chord on the 1st degree and, as a substitute, the chord on the 6th degree (vi), which shares two notes with the tonic. The iii chord can act as a tonic or as a dominant depending on the context, so it is not asked.
Which chords have a dominant function?
The chord on the 5th degree (also with a seventh, V7) and the chord on the 7th degree (vii°), which has the leading tone and the tritone and asks to resolve to the tonic.
Does the function change if the chord is inverted?
No. The function depends on the scale degree the chord is built on, not on the note in the bass. V in first inversion is still a dominant.
Which keys are practiced?
Seven major keys: C, G, D, F, B♭, A and E♭, each with its own key signature. Level 1 uses only the first four.
