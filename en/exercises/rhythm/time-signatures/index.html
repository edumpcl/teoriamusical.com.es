Time Signature Exercises

Exercises to practice time signatures: understanding what a time signature says, completing measures that are missing a note, recognizing the time signature from the written rhythm, finding syncopation and finding off-beat notes. Answers are checked right away.

The exercises

  1. Analyze time signatures: you see a time signature (2/4, 6/8, 9/16, 3/2…) and say whether it is simple or compound, how many beats it has, how it subdivides, and what its beat, subdivision and measure units are.
  2. Complete the measure: a measure of 2/4, 3/4, 4/4, 6/8, 9/8 or 12/8 is missing something at a marked spot, and you say which note, which rest or which notes are missing. Three levels.
  3. Recognize the time signature: you see a measure written without its time signature and tap the top and bottom numbers that belong to it. When the rhythm can be written in more than one way (3/4 and 6/8 last the same), both are accepted.
  4. Syncopation: you see a rhythmic excerpt and tap the note where the syncopation starts (or "no syncopation" if there is none), telling it apart from a long tied note and from an off-beat note.
  5. Off-beat notes: you see a rhythmic excerpt and tap the note that is off the beat (or "no off-beat" if there is none), telling it apart from a normal entrance and from true syncopation.

To recognize the time signature by ear, without seeing the score, there is the ear-training exercise recognize the time signature by ear.

The recommended order is the one above: first understand the time signature, then complete measures, and finally recognize it from the rhythm when it is not written. In between, the note value and rhythm exercises help, especially the value of note values in each measure and adding up the note values in a measure.

The theory is in the dictionary: time signatures, simple and compound meter and beat unit and measure unit. To internalize the pulse of each meter, use the online metronome.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which time signature exercises are there?
Five: analyze time signatures, where you see a time signature and say whether it is simple or compound, its beats and its units; complete the measure, where a measure is missing a note or a rest and you say which; recognize the time signature, where you see the written rhythm without its time signature and work it out; syncopation, where you tap on the staff the note where the syncopation starts; and off-beat notes, where you tap the note that is off the beat. All five are checked right away.
Which time signatures are included?
In analyze time signatures, twenty different time signatures, from common to uncommon ones (2/1, 9/16…). In complete the measure and recognize the time signature, the six most common: 2/4, 3/4, 4/4, 6/8, 9/8 and 12/8.
