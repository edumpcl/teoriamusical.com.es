Quick guide: how to analyze a time signature

Simple vs. compound meter

A meter is simple when each beat divides into two equal parts (binary subdivision). It is compound when each beat divides into three (ternary subdivision). The practical rule: if the top number is 2, 3 or 4, the meter is simple; if it is 6, 9 or 12, it is compound. (This is a rule of thumb: for instance 3/8 and 6/8 follow it, while irregular meters such as 5/4 or 7/8 are a separate case.)

Number of beats

In simple meters the top number is the number of beats. In compound meters, divide the top number by 3: 6/8 has 2 beats, 9/8 has 3 and 12/8 has 4.

Units

The beat unit is the note value that lasts one beat. In simple meters it matches the bottom number (4 = quarter note, 2 = half note, 8 = eighth note). In compound meters the bottom number gives the note value that is grouped in threes (for example, the eighth note when the bottom number is 8), and the beat unit is the next larger note value with a dot (a dotted quarter note when the bottom number is 8). The subdivision unit is half the beat unit in simple meters, and the note value of the bottom number itself in compound meters. The measure unit is the note value that lasts the whole measure (UK: bar): you get it by multiplying the beat unit by the number of beats.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I tell whether a time signature is simple or compound?
Look at how the beat divides. If each beat splits into two equal parts, the meter is simple; if it splits into three, it is compound. As a quick rule, a top number of 2, 3 or 4 means simple, and 6, 9 or 12 means compound.
How many beats does 6/8 have?
Two. In compound meters you divide the top number by 3, because the eighth notes are grouped in threes: 6/8 has 2 beats, 9/8 has 3 and 12/8 has 4. The beat unit is the dotted quarter note.
What are the beat unit, subdivision unit and measure unit?
The beat unit is the note value that lasts one beat. The subdivision unit is the note value into which the beat divides (half the beat unit in simple meters, a third in compound meters). The measure unit is the single note value that lasts the whole measure, such as a dotted half note in 3/4.
