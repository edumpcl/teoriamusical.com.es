Which Time Signature Is It?

This exercise shows a measure full of tuplets (triplets, duplets, quadruplets, sextuplets), without its time signature, and you must work it out. You solve it by simplifying: seeing which simple note value each irregular group equals and adding up. Three levels, from a single group inside the measure to a whole measure made of groups.

Choose the top number and the bottom number that match the measure you see: to get it right, first reduce each tuplet in your head to the duration it stands for.

How to "simplify" a measure with irregular groups

  1. Identify each group by its bracket and its number: 3 is a triplet, 2 a duplet, 4 a quadruplet, 6 a sextuplet.
  2. Replace it in your head with the simple note value it stands for in this exercise (a triplet of eighth notes becomes a quarter note, a duplet of eighth notes becomes a dotted quarter note, and so on, following the equivalence table).
  3. Add up everything that is written: the simplified groups and the notes that are not part of any group. Count how many beats it fills, and that gives the time signature.

This kind of exercise makes you reduce the irregular note values in your head before you recognize the metric structure. The more beats of the measure are taken up by groups, the harder it is to "see" the time signature at a glance, and the more you need to simplify calmly.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you recognize the time signature when there are tuplets?
By simplifying: in this exercise, each tuplet (triplet, duplet, quadruplet, sextuplet) is reduced to the simple note value it stands for. You replace each group in your head with that note value, add up everything that is written and count how many beats the measure fills.
Can there be more than one irregular group in the same measure?
Yes. At the easy level there is a single group; at the medium level, two; at the hard level, the whole measure is made of irregular groups, with no ordinary note values at all.
How do I know which time signature it is when there are triplets or other tuplets?
Simplify each group to the note value it stands for in this exercise (a triplet of eighth notes becomes a quarter note, a duplet of eighth notes becomes a dotted quarter note, and so on) and add up all the resulting note values: that total tells you how many beats the measure fills. A 4/4 always also accepts 2/2, and a 2/4 always also accepts 4/8, because in each case both time signatures fit exactly the same. Apart from those two cases, unlike recognize the time signature, other alternative readings are not checked here.
Which time signatures are in the exercise?
The six common ones: 2/4, 3/4, 4/4, 6/8, 9/8 and 12/8.
