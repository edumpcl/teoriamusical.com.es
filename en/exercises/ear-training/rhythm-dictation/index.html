Rhythmic Dictation

Rhythmic dictation means listening to a rhythm and writing it down: which notes sound, where the rests are, and in which measure (UK: bar). This is a collection of exercises organized by note values, from the easiest to the hardest. Each group trains the same material in three ways: recognize the rhythm you hear among four written ones, read a rhythm and recognize its audio, and write it yourself.

The dictations are generated on the spot, played on the note G on the piano and checked instantly. Turn up the volume or use headphones. Note values are named the American way (quarter note, eighth note); the first time a page uses a name that differs in British English, it gives the British name in brackets, for example quarter note (UK: crotchet).

The dictations

Quarter notes and quarter rests

One beat, one figure: two measures of 2/4, 3/4 or 4/4 with quarter notes and quarter rests. This is the first step: learning to keep the pulse inside and to count the rests.

The three exercises in each group train the same skill from two directions: from sound to score (recognize and write) and from score to sound (read). If you make many mistakes in the dictation, go back to the first two: it is the best way to find out whether the problem is hearing the rhythm or writing it.

Frequently asked questions

What is a rhythmic dictation?
It means listening to a rhythm and writing it: which notes and rests sound, inside their measures. It is practiced from the first years of music theory, first with quarter notes and quarter rests, then with half notes, dotted half notes and whole notes, and then, with all of them, pairs of eighth notes, groups of four sixteenth notes and the eighth rest followed by an eighth note.
In what order should I do the exercises?
Each group of note values has three. First, which rhythm is playing: you hear the dictation and recognize it among four written rhythms. Then, which audio is it: you read a rhythm and recognize it among four audios. Last, the dictation itself: you write it with cards, with no options.
What do I need to do them?
Only a browser and sound: speakers or, better, headphones. You do not need a microphone or to install anything.

To keep working on written rhythm, try the note value exercises and completing measures; and for your ear, the rest of the ear training exercises.
