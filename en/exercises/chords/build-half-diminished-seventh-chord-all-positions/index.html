Build Half-Diminished Seventh Chords: All Positions

You are given one note of a half-diminished (leading-tone) seventh chord (chosen at random) and place the other three on the staff, in whichever position the question asks for: root position or any of the three inversions. The chord is half-diminished (leading-tone) seventh: m3 + d5 + m7 above the root. The question shows which note of the chord you are given, and which ones you have to add depend on the inversion that comes up.

Structure of the half-diminished (leading-tone) seventh chord

The half-diminished seventh chord, also called the leading-tone seventh chord, is built on the seventh degree (the leading tone) of a major key. It is a diminished triad with a minor seventh (not a diminished one) added on top, so it is not symmetrical like the fully diminished seventh chord.

First inversion6/5m3 + P5 + M6

Exercise

How to use the exercise: Click (or drag) on the staff to place each note. If a note needs an accidental, choose it first with the accidental buttons (double flat, flat, natural, sharp, double sharp). Undo removes the last note and Clear restarts the exercise. The magnifier shows the note you are about to place at a larger size.

In every inversion the chord still contains the same four notes. If you get stuck, find the interval of a second (or its inversion, a seventh) among the notes: the root is the upper note of that second when the chord is written in close position.

Other positions of the half-diminished (leading-tone) seventh chord: root position, first inversion, second inversion, third inversion. You can also practice recognizing them (together with Dominant seventh and Diminished seventh chords in the same position) or review the theory on the half-diminished (leading-tone) seventh chord page.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a half-diminished (leading-tone) seventh chord?
The half-diminished seventh chord, also called the leading-tone seventh chord, is built on the seventh degree (the leading tone) of a major key. It is a diminished triad with a minor seventh (not a diminished one) added on top, so it is not symmetrical like the fully diminished seventh chord.
How is the half-diminished (leading-tone) seventh chord labeled in each position?
In root position it is labeled ø7. The inversions use figured bass: 6/5 (first inversion), 4/3 (second inversion) and 4/2 (third inversion). The numbers are the intervals counted from the bass note.
Which note is given and which ones do I have to draw?
One note of the chord is given on the staff, together with the chord type and the position. You draw the other three. Which note is given changes from question to question.
Why is a double accidental sometimes needed?
Depending on the starting note, one of the chord notes may need a double sharp or a double flat to keep the correct letter name (for example, a half-diminished seventh chord on D♭ contains A𝄫). This appears mostly on the hard level.
