Build Half-Diminished Seventh Chords in First Inversion

You are given the third (in the bass) of a half-diminished (leading-tone) seventh chord (m3 + d5 + m7 above the root), in first inversion. Your task is to place the other three notes of the chord on the staff: the fifth, seventh and root.

Structure of the half-diminished (leading-tone) seventh chord

The half-diminished seventh chord, also called the leading-tone seventh chord, is built on the seventh degree (the leading tone) of a major key. It is a diminished triad with a minor seventh (not a diminished one) added on top, so it is not symmetrical like the fully diminished seventh chord.

Exercise

How to use the exercise: Click (or drag) on the staff to place each note. If a note needs an accidental, choose it first with the accidental buttons (double flat, flat, natural, sharp, double sharp). Undo removes the last note and Clear restarts the exercise. The magnifier shows the note you are about to place at a larger size.

In every inversion the chord still contains the same four notes. If you get stuck, find the interval of a second (or its inversion, a seventh) among the notes: the root is the upper note of that second when the chord is written in close position.

Other positions of the half-diminished (leading-tone) seventh chord: root position, second inversion, third inversion, all positions mixed. You can also practice recognizing them (together with Dominant seventh and Diminished seventh chords in the same position) or review the theory on the half-diminished (leading-tone) seventh chord page.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a half-diminished (leading-tone) seventh chord?
The half-diminished seventh chord, also called the leading-tone seventh chord, is built on the seventh degree (the leading tone) of a major key. It is a diminished triad with a minor seventh (not a diminished one) added on top, so it is not symmetrical like the fully diminished seventh chord.
How is the half-diminished (leading-tone) seventh chord labeled in first inversion?
It is labeled 6/5, with the intervals m3 + P5 + M6 counted from the bass note.
Which note is given and which ones do I have to draw?
The third is given on the staff (in the bass). You draw the other three: the fifth, seventh and root.
Why is a double accidental sometimes needed?
Depending on the starting note, one of the chord notes may need a double sharp or a double flat to keep the correct letter name (for example, a half-diminished seventh chord on D♭ contains A𝄫). This appears mostly on the hard level.
