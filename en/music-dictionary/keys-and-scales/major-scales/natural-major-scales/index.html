Natural Major Scales

The major scale is the most basic scale of Western music. Every major scale has eight notes in a row (seven different notes, then the octave) and follows the same pattern of steps: whole step, whole step, half step, whole step, whole step, whole step, half step (UK: tone, tone, semitone, tone, tone, tone, semitone). Start that pattern on any note and you get the major scale of that note. On this page we call it the natural major scale to tell it apart from the variants that change one or two of its notes: the harmonic major, Mixolydian and melodic major scales. Each of the seven notes is a scale degree with its own name and function (tonic, dominant, leading tone...).

A scale can be written in two ways: with the accidentals written next to the notes inside the scale, or with a key signature at the start of the staff (UK: stave). Both ways sound the same. Exercises usually ask for one of them, so practice both.

Order of the keys with sharps

  1. G major has 1 sharp.
  2. D major has 2 sharps.
  3. A major has 3 sharps.
  4. E major has 4 sharps.
  5. B major has 5 sharps.
  6. F♯ major has 6 sharps.
  7. C♯ major has 7 sharps.

Each new key with sharps is found by going up a perfect 5th from the previous tonic, starting on C (C, G, D, A, E, B, F♯, C♯). It adds one sharp, always on the new leading tone.

Order of the keys with flats

  1. F major has 1 flat.
  2. B♭ major has 2 flats.
  3. E♭ major has 3 flats.
  4. A♭ major has 4 flats.
  5. D♭ major has 5 flats.
  6. G♭ major has 6 flats.
  7. C♭ major has 7 flats.

Each new key with flats is found by going down a perfect 5th from the previous tonic, starting on C (C, F, B♭, E♭, A♭, D♭, G♭, C♭). It adds one flat. Three pairs of keys sound the same although they are written differently (B major and C♭ major, F♯ major and G♭ major, C♯ major and D♭ major); this is an example of enharmony.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is a major scale built?
A major scale is built by applying the pattern whole step, whole step, half step, whole step, whole step, whole step, half step from the tonic. These seven steps give the eight notes of any major scale.
How many major scales are there?
Fifteen major scales are usually written out: C major (no accidentals), seven with sharps (G, D, A, E, B, F♯, C♯) and seven with flats (F, B♭, E♭, A♭, D♭, G♭, C♭). Because B, F♯ and C♯ major sound the same as C♭, G♭ and D♭ major, there are only 12 different major scales by sound. Conservatories ask for all 15 to practice reading key signatures.
How many sharps or flats does each major key have?
G major has 1 sharp, D major 2, A major 3, E major 4, B major 5, F♯ major 6 and C♯ major 7. For flats: F major has 1, B♭ major 2, E♭ major 3, A♭ major 4, D♭ major 5, G♭ major 6 and C♭ major 7.
What are the notes of the G major scale?
G A B C D E F♯. It is the first key with sharps and has only one, F♯. Applying the pattern whole, whole, half, whole, whole, whole, half from G gives exactly these seven notes.
How do I build a major scale step by step?
Choose the tonic and apply the pattern: whole step (2 half steps), whole step, half step (1 half step), whole step, whole step, whole step, half step. For example, from D: D, E (whole step), F♯ (whole step), G (half step), A (whole step), B (whole step), C♯ (whole step), D (half step). A scale uses seven different letter names, one for each degree.
