Harmonic Major Scales

The harmonic major scale is a variant of the natural major scale with a single change: the sixth degree is lowered a half step (UK: semitone). That alteration gives the scale a distinctive dark color without leaving the major sound: the tonic triad is still major.

Other names for the same scale are Ionian ♭6 (the major scale, or Ionian mode, with a lowered 6th) and, in Spanish conservatory teaching, escala mayor mixta principal ("main mixed major scale"). "Mixed" refers to the mix of major and minor features: a major scale that uses the sixth degree of the parallel minor. In English the usual name is harmonic major, by analogy with harmonic minor: both have an augmented second between degrees 6 and 7.

Interval pattern

The pattern of whole steps and half steps of the harmonic major scale is W–W–H–W–H–A2–H (W = whole step, H = half step, A2 = augmented second, which is 3 half steps). The lowered sixth degree creates an augmented second between degrees 6 and 7, the same interval that appears in the harmonic minor scale.

Compare it with C harmonic minor (C D E♭ F G A♭ B). The two scales share the lowered sixth, the leading tone and the augmented second. They differ only in the third degree: E in harmonic major, E♭ in harmonic minor.

Chords built on this scale explain its appeal. The scale contains a C major triad (I), an F minor triad (iv, with A♭) and a G dominant seventh (G-B-D-F), so it fits a I–iv–V–I progression with a minor subdominant, a classic "borrowed chord" color.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the harmonic major scale?
The harmonic major scale is a variant of the major scale in which the sixth degree is lowered a half step. This gives it a special color that clearly sets it apart from the natural major scale, and an augmented second between the sixth and seventh degrees.
How does the harmonic major scale differ from the natural major scale?
The only difference is the sixth degree, which is lowered a half step. For example, C major has A natural, while C harmonic major has A♭.
How does harmonic major differ from the Mixolydian scale?
The Mixolydian scale lowers only the seventh degree, while harmonic major lowers only the sixth. Mixolydian has no leading tone and sounds more modal. Harmonic major keeps the leading tone and has an augmented second between degrees 6 and 7, which gives it a darker, more exotic color. Both share the tonic and the other degrees with the natural major scale.
What are the notes of C harmonic major?
C harmonic major has the notes C-D-E-F-G-A♭-B. The only difference from C major is the A♭ in the sixth degree (C major has A).
Is harmonic major the same as harmonic minor?
No. They share the lowered sixth, the leading tone and the augmented second, but harmonic major has a major third (C D E ...) and harmonic minor has a minor third (C D E♭ ...), so one sounds major and the other minor.
Why is it called "mixed main" in Spanish conservatories?
Spanish conservatory teaching calls it "mixta principal" to tell it apart from the Mixolydian scale (lowered 7th) and from the "mixta secundaria", or melodic major scale (lowered 6th and 7th). The name shows that only one degree, the sixth, is altered, whereas the secondary one alters two.
