Major Scales

The major scale is the most important scale in Western music: the series of seven notes on which most melodies and harmonies are built, in classical and popular music alike. This page explains what it is, how it is formed with its pattern of whole steps (UK: tones) and half steps (UK: semitones), and what the fifteen major scales and their key signatures are. Further down you will also find the four types of major scale studied in conservatories.

What is a major scale?

A major scale is an ordered series of seven notes (plus the octave, which repeats the tonic) separated by a fixed pattern of whole steps and half steps. That pattern gives the major scale its bright, happy character. It stays the same wherever you start, so there is a major scale starting on each of the twelve notes. It helps to be clear about intervals and the names of the scale degrees before you go on.

Can you tell by ear whether a melody is in a major or a minor key, without looking at the score? Test your ear with the listening exercise Major or minor?

Structure: the pattern of whole steps and half steps

Every natural major scale follows this pattern of intervals between consecutive degrees:

Whole · Whole · Half · Whole · Whole · Whole · Half

That is, the two half steps always fall between degrees 3–4 and 7–8; every other step is a whole step. In C major, the only major scale with no accidentals, the pattern is easy to see: C–D (whole), D–E (whole), E–F (half), F–G (whole), G–A (whole), A–B (whole) and B–C (half).

How to build a major scale from any note

  1. Write the seven natural note names starting on the chosen tonic, in order and without repeating any letter (one for each degree).
  2. Measure the distance between each pair of consecutive notes and compare it with the pattern W–W–H–W–W–W–H.
  3. Add the sharps or flats needed so that every step matches the pattern exactly.

For example, on G: G–A–B–C–D–E–F–G. Every step fits except the last one: F–G would be a whole step, but the pattern needs a half step up to the tonic. So F is raised to F♯, and the key signature of one sharp appears. The result is G major: G–A–B–C–D–E–F♯.

The 15 major scales and their key signatures

There are only twelve different pitches, but in written music fifteen major scales are used. Three of them can be written in two enharmonic ways (B/C♭, F♯/G♭ and C♯/D♭). Each scale has its own key signature, whose sharps or flats appear in the order of sharps and flats.

Could you recognize them by ear? In the listening exercise recognize the major key you hear a reference A4 and a melody, and you have to say which major key it is in. When you master that, try telling them apart from their relative minors in recognize the key: major and minor.

The 4 types of major scale in conservatories

Besides the natural major scale described above, Spanish conservatories study four types of major scale: the natural major, the Mixolydian, the "principal mixed" (called the harmonic major in most English-language sources) and the "secondary mixed" (the melodic major scale, also known as Mixolydian ♭6, Aeolian dominant or Hindu scale). Each variant starts from the natural major scale and lowers one or two degrees to get a different sound color. Frequently Asked Questions

How many types of major scales are there?
Conservatory study covers four types: the natural major (W-W-H-W-W-W-H), the Mixolydian (lowered 7th), the principal mixed or harmonic major (lowered 6th) and the secondary mixed or Mixolydian ♭6 (lowered 6th and 7th). Each has a different sound character.
What is the difference between the natural major and the Mixolydian scale?
The natural major and the Mixolydian are identical except for the seventh degree: in the Mixolydian that degree is lowered by a half step. That single change gives the Mixolydian its characteristic modal, dominant-seventh sound.
What is a mixed major scale?
The mixed major scales are variants of the natural major that change one or more degrees. The principal mixed (harmonic major) lowers the 6th degree, which creates an augmented 2nd between the 6th and 7th degrees. The secondary mixed lowers both the 6th and the 7th degrees.
How many notes does a major scale have?
Every major scale has 7 different notes plus the octave, which repeats the tonic an octave higher. The pattern W-W-H-W-W-W-H (whole, whole, half, whole, whole, whole, half) applies from any tonic to give the natural major scale.
Why are 15 major scales studied and not 12?
There are 12 different pitches in the chromatic scale, but in notation 15 major scales are used because C♯/D♭, F♯/G♭ and B/C♭ can be written in two equivalent enharmonic ways. Conservatory students practice all 15 to master every key signature with sharps and with flats.
