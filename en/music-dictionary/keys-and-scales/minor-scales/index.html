Minor Scales

The minor scale is, together with the major, one of the two fundamental scales of tonal music. Its darker, more expressive sound comes from having a minor 3rd above the tonic. This page explains what it is, its pattern of whole steps (UK: tones) and half steps (UK: semitones), its relationship with the relative major scale and the fifteen minor scales with their key signatures. Further down you will find the four types of minor scale studied in conservatories.

What is a minor scale?

A minor scale is an ordered series of seven notes (plus the octave) separated by a pattern of whole steps and half steps that differs from the major one. The essential difference is in the third degree: in the minor scale it is a minor 3rd (a half step closer to the tonic than in the major), which gives it its introspective character. The basic form is the natural minor (or Aeolian), from which the other types are obtained by altering some degree.

Can you tell by ear whether a melody is in a major or a minor key, without looking at the score? Test your ear with the listening exercise Major or minor?

Structure: the pattern of the natural minor scale

The natural minor scale follows this pattern of intervals between consecutive degrees:

Whole · Half · Whole · Whole · Half · Whole · Whole

The two half steps fall between degrees 2–3 and 5–6. In A minor, the only minor scale with no accidentals, it is easy to see: A–B (whole), B–C (half), C–D (whole), D–E (whole), E–F (half), F–G (whole) and G–A (whole).

The relative minor: same key signature as the major

Every natural minor scale has exactly the same notes as a major scale, and therefore the same key signature: it is its relative minor. The tonic of the relative minor is a minor 3rd below the major tonic, that is, on its sixth degree. So A minor is the relative of C major (both with no accidentals), E minor the relative of G major, and so on. Recognizing this relationship is key to identifying the key and to working with closely related keys.

The 15 minor scales and their key signatures

As in the major mode, fifteen natural minor scales are used, each with the key signature of its relative major. Three tonics can be written in two enharmonic ways (G♯/A♭, D♯/E♭ and A♯/B♭). The sharps or flats appear in the order of sharps and flats.

Could you recognize them by ear? In the listening exercise recognize the minor key you hear a reference A4 and a melody, and you have to say which minor key it is in. When you master that, try telling them apart from their relative majors in recognize the key: major and minor.

The 4 types of minor scale in conservatories

Starting from the natural minor scale, conservatories study four types of minor scale: the natural (or Aeolian), the harmonic, the melodic and the Dorian. Each raises one or two degrees, either to strengthen the cadence or to smooth the melody. Frequently Asked Questions

What is a minor scale?
A minor scale is a series of 8 pitches (with the octave) that follows a characteristic pattern of intervals. Unlike the major scale, it has a minor 3rd above the tonic, which gives it its darker, more expressive sound.
How many types of minor scales are there?
There are four main types of minor scales: the natural (or Aeolian), the harmonic, the melodic and the Dorian. Each has a different pattern of intervals and a different musical character.
How is a minor scale different from a major scale?
The main difference is in the third degree: the minor scale has a minor 3rd (a half step smaller than the major). This gives it a darker, more introspective sound compared with the bright, happy sound of the major scale.
Which degrees distinguish the types of minor scale?
The natural minor scale changes no degree relative to the key signature. The harmonic raises the 7th degree (the leading tone, UK: leading note). The melodic raises the 6th and 7th going up and goes down like the natural minor. The Dorian raises only the 6th degree.
How many minor scales are studied in conservatories?
Fifteen minor scales of each type (natural, harmonic, melodic and Dorian), one for each key. Students practice them to master every key signature with sharps and with flats and to recognize the characteristic leading tone of the harmonic minor.

The minor scale is only one of many: discover them all in types of musical scales.
