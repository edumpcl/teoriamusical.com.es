Performance Effects

Performance effects are techniques that change how sounds are produced, connected or repeated: sliding from one note to another, playing a chord note by note, making the pitch of a long note waver, or repeating a note very fast. Some are marked with signs or words in the score, such as glissando, arpeggiato and tremolo; others depend more on the conventions of the instrument, the style or the performer’s choices, such as portamento and vibrato. “Performance effects” is a teaching category: the boundaries between them, and with ornaments, are not absolute.

Glissando

The glissando connects two notes with a slide of pitch. The result depends on the instrument: on the voice, the violin or the slide trombone the pitch can change continuously; on the piano the effect is made by quickly running over a series of keys. Its own page shows how it is written, examples you can listen to, and the difference from portamento.

Portamento

The portamento is a short, expressive drag between two notes, typical of singing and of bowed strings, and often left to the performer. It is similar to glissando but shorter and smoother. Its own page lets you hear the difference between a direct leap, a portamento and a glissando.

Arpeggiato

The arpeggiato (a rolled or spread chord) spreads out the notes of a chord: they enter one after another, normally from lowest to highest (although it can also go downward), and keep sounding. It is written with a wavy line before the chord. It is not the same as an arpeggio, in which each note is played with its own duration.

Vibrato

The vibrato is a periodic oscillation of pitch around a note, normally about five to seven times per second, which adds expressiveness to a long note: although the pitch fluctuates slightly, the note is still heard as the same. It is typical of the voice and of bowed strings.

Tremolo

The tremolo repeats the same note, or alternates two, at high speed, and is written with slashes across the stem. Do not confuse it with vibrato: vibrato moves the pitch; tremolo re-attacks the note again and again.

Performance effects and ornaments

The ornaments (appoggiatura, acciaccatura, mordent, turn and trill) embellish the melody with ornamental notes or formulas. Performance effects focus on how the sound is produced: sliding between pitches, spreading a chord, wavering a note or repeating sounds very fast. The distinction is not absolute: some techniques can be classified differently depending on the instrument, the tradition or the context (the glissando, for example, is sometimes listed among ornaments). To study the signs and function of ornaments, see also the page on musical ornaments.

To practice

You can practice pitch slides with your voice in the javelin throw game, in which you slide your voice from one note to another at constant speed. To understand the distance between the starting and ending notes, also review musical intervals.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are performance effects?
They are techniques that change how sounds are produced, connected or repeated: sliding from one note to another (glissando and portamento), playing a chord note by note (arpeggiato), making the pitch waver (vibrato) or repeating a note very fast (tremolo). Some are marked in the score and others depend on the conventions of the instrument or the performer.
What is the difference between glissando and portamento?
Both can describe a slide between two notes. Glissando usually stresses the path between the pitches and is often written in the score; portamento usually refers to a more expressive link that is often left to the performer. Their meanings overlap on some instruments and in some traditions.
What is the difference between an ornament and a performance effect?
Ornaments (appoggiatura, mordent, turn, trill) embellish the melody with ornamental notes or formulas. Performance effects focus on how the sound is produced. The distinction is not absolute: some techniques are classified differently depending on the instrument or the tradition.
What is the difference between vibrato and tremolo?
Vibrato is a small, regular oscillation of the pitch of a sustained note. Tremolo is the very fast repetition of one note, or fast alternation between two. The first moves the pitch; the second re-attacks the note.
What is an arpeggiated (rolled) chord?
It is a chord whose notes are played one after another, normally from lowest to highest, instead of sounding together. It is written with a vertical wavy line before the chord.
