Euphonium Notes

The euphonium (in Spanish, bombardino) is the warm baritone of the brass family: a conical-bore instrument in B♭, related to the tuba but higher, with a round, singing sound. It is one of the leading voices of the concert band, especially in the Valencian tradition. Like the trumpet, it picks a note by combining valves with the harmonics, but it sounds much lower and usually has four valves. In Spanish bands, and in many US bands, it is read at concert pitch in bass clef (it does not transpose). This page gives its notes and fingerings.

Euphonium or baritone horn? The Spanish bombardino is the euphonium. In the US, the baritone horn (often just "baritone") is a different, smaller instrument: also in B♭ and fingered the same way, but with a narrower bore, usually three valves and a lighter sound. The German Baritonhorn is another narrow-bore instrument. Do not confuse bombardon either: in French and German it means a tuba, not a euphonium.

How are the notes produced? Valves and harmonics

The sound starts at the lips, which vibrate against the mouthpiece and produce a series of harmonics. The valves fill the gaps between those harmonics: each one adds tubing and lowers the pitch, as on the trumpet, but starting from B♭.

For example, the open harmonics of the B♭ euphonium include B♭2, F3, B♭3, D4, F4 and B♭4. Press the 4th valve on F3 and you get C3, a perfect 4th lower.

The fourth valve: the low register

The 4th valve is usually placed at the side and pressed with the left hand on many models. It lowers the instrument by a perfect 4th. It does the same job as 1st + 3rd, but it is better in tune: the 1st + 3rd combination leaves the tubing slightly short, so the note tends to sound sharp. Many four-valve euphoniums are compensating: the extra tubing keeps the low valve combinations in tune. The 4th valve is the key to the low register: combined with the other three valves it reaches the notes below the low E (the table below goes down to B1, with the pedal tone B♭1 below that) and gives alternate fingerings that are better in tune. Three-valve euphoniums do not go as low.

Euphonium, bombardino and the band

In Spain the instrument is called bombardino; in English-speaking countries and most others, euphonium. It is the same instrument: a conical-bore brass in B♭ related to the tuba. It should not be confused with the trombone (cylindrical bore and brighter sound) or with the tuba (larger and lower). In the concert band, the backbone of the Valencian tradition, the euphonium shines in the lyrical melodies of the tenor-baritone register and reinforces the bass line with the tuba. In the British brass band it is also a leading solo voice.

Is the euphonium a transposing instrument?

It depends on the tradition, and this is the most common source of confusion:

  • Bass clef, concert pitch. In Spanish bands, in orchestral parts and in many US concert-band parts, the euphonium is read in bass clef exactly as it sounds. It does not transpose. This page uses that notation.
  • Treble clef, in B♭. In the British brass band tradition (and in many US band parts, which often supply both versions) the euphonium is written in treble clef as a B♭ transposing instrument, a major 9th below the written note (an octave plus a whole step). A written C4 sounds B♭2.

It is the same instrument and the same fingerings. A given fingering always gives the same sounding pitch; only the notation changes: the treble-clef player reads a note a major 9th higher on the page than the bass-clef player for the same sound.

Common mistakes when learning euphonium notes

  • Thinking there is one fingering per note. As on the trumpet, there are few combinations: the same one serves several notes, depending on the harmonic.
  • Forgetting the 4th valve in the low register. The lowest notes need it (alone or combined); without it they do not sound or are out of tune.
  • Mixing up the clefs. In Spanish bands the part is in bass clef at concert pitch; in the British tradition it is in treble clef, transposed. They are different systems for the same instrument.
  • Neglecting the air. The euphonium needs a lot of airflow; its singing sound is sustained by the air column, not by forcing the lips.

Summary

  • The euphonium (bombardino) is the conical baritone of the brass in B♭: valves plus harmonics, like the trumpet but lower, and with 4 valves.
  • In Spanish bands and many US bands it is read at concert pitch in bass clef (no transposition); in the British tradition it is in treble clef, a major 9th lower than written.
  • The 4th valve (a perfect 4th lower, usually at the side) opens the low register.
  • It is not the same as the US baritone horn, which is smaller and narrower.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are the bombardino and the euphonium the same instrument?
Yes, in practice: the Spanish bombardino is what other countries call the euphonium. It is a conical-bore brass instrument in B♭, in the tuba family but higher. It is not the same as the US baritone horn, which is a smaller, narrower-bore instrument usually with three valves.
Does the euphonium transpose?
It depends on the tradition. In Spanish bands, in orchestral parts and in many US band parts, the euphonium is read at concert pitch in bass clef: it does not transpose. In the British brass band tradition, it is written in treble clef as a B♭ transposing instrument and sounds a major 9th (an octave plus a whole step) lower than written. This page uses concert pitch in bass clef.
What is the fourth valve of the euphonium for?
The 4th valve lowers the sound by a perfect 4th (it equals 1st + 3rd but is better in tune). It lets you reach the low notes below the low E and gives more in-tune alternate fingerings. Three-valve euphoniums do not go as low.
How many valves does the euphonium have?
There are three-valve and four-valve models. The four-valve one (often compensating, with the 4th valve at the side, pressed with the left hand) is the professional standard and the one that opens the whole low register. The fingering system is the same as the trumpet's, but in B♭ and with that fourth valve.
