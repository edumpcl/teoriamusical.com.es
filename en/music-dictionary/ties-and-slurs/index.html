Ties and Slurs

A tie or slur is a curved line that connects two or more notes in a score. They are drawn in a similar way, but there are two very different types: the tie and the slur. Telling them apart is essential, because one affects duration and the other affects performance.

The tie

The tie joins two notes of the same pitch (the same note) and adds their durations into one sustained sound: the second note is not played again, the first one is simply held longer. It is mostly used to hold a note across the bar line of a measure (UK: bar) or to get lengths that a single note value cannot write.

The slur

The slur joins notes of different pitches and tells the performer to play them smoothly connected, with no break between them. The Italian word for this is legato. A slur does not change the length of the notes. It is a mark of articulation and phrasing (a phrase mark is a longer slur that shows a musical phrase).

Rule of thumb: if the curve joins two identical notes, it is a tie (one sustained note sounds). If it joins different notes, it is a slur (they are played connected, without changing their length).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a tie or slur in music?
It is a curved line that connects two or more notes. There are two types: the tie, which joins notes of the same pitch and adds their duration, and the slur, which joins different notes so they are played smoothly connected (legato).
What is the difference between a tie and a slur?
A tie joins two notes of the same pitch and adds their durations into one sound. A slur joins notes of different pitches and tells the performer to play them connected (legato), without changing their length.
Does a tie change the length of the note?
Yes. A tie adds the durations of the two joined notes into a single sustained sound. The second note is not played again; the first one is just held longer.
What is legato?
Legato is the smooth, connected way of playing a group of notes, marked with a slur. It is the opposite of staccato, where notes are played short and separated. You can see them alongside the other signs on the page about articulation.
How do I know if a curve is a tie or a slur?
Look at the notes it joins. If they have the same pitch (the same note), it is a tie and one sustained sound is heard. If they have different pitches, it is a slur and the notes are played connected.
