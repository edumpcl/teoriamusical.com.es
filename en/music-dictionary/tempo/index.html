Tempo in Music

Tempo is the speed at which a piece is performed, that is, how fast the beat (pulse) goes. Along with dynamics, it is one of the elements that most changes the character of a piece: the same melody sounds solemn when played slowly and cheerful when played fast. Tempo is shown in two ways: with Italian terms and with a metronome mark. For example, Allegro is about 115–129 bpm and Andante about 76–89 bpm. In the chart below you can hear each tempo by pressing a button.

The metronome mark (BPM)

This is the most exact way: a note value set equal to a number of beats per minute (BPM). For example, ♩ = 120 means 120 quarter notes (UK: crotchets) per minute. You can hear and adjust any tempo with our online metronome.

Tempo markings

The BPM ranges are approximate guides, not hard borders. The exact limits vary with the period, the composer and the character of the piece, and each edition uses slightly different ones. Markings are often adjusted with adverbs and adjectives: molto (very), poco (a little), assai (quite, very), ma non troppo (but not too much), con moto (with motion) or con brio (with spirit).

Combined tempo terms: Allegro vivace, ma non troppo, Andante moderato…

In scores these terms rarely appear alone. They are combined with the modifiers above to fine-tune speed and character (character is shown by the expression terms, such as dolce or maestoso). These are the most common combinations:

MarkingMeaningSpeed compared with the base term
Allegro ma non troppoFast, but not too muchA little more restrained than Allegro
Allegro moderatoFast, but moderateBetween Moderato and Allegro
Allegro assaiVery fastFaster than Allegro
Allegro vivaceFast and livelyFaster than Allegro, toward Vivace
Allegro con brioFast and with spirit (energy)Allegro with an energetic character
Andante moderato"Walking", a little more movementBetween Andante and Moderato
Andante con moto (or mosso)Andante with more motionA little faster than Andante
Più mosso / Meno mossoMore motion / less motionA change relative to the previous tempo

The key is in the modifiers: assai and vivace push toward faster, while ma non troppo, moderato and meno mosso pull toward more restrained. That is why an Allegro assai is livelier than an Allegro ma non troppo, even though both start from the same Allegro.

Tempo changes (agogics)

Tempo is not always fixed. It can speed up (accelerando), slow down (ritardando, rallentando or ritenuto), return to the earlier speed (a tempo), change at once to another (più mosso, meno mosso), stop on a fermata or become flexible with rubato. All of this is the subject of agogics.

That page explains what is most often confused: ritardando slows gradually while ritenuto slows at once, and più mosso is not an accelerando but a new tempo starting on that very note.

To practice all this there are tempo and agogics exercises: what each term means, ordering speeds from slowest to fastest, and a PDF worksheet with answers.

Tempo applies to the beat that organizes each measure (UK: bar), and you work on it with the metronome to internalize a steady speed before adding expressive flexibility.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is tempo in music?
Tempo is the speed at which a piece is performed, that is, how fast the beat goes. It is shown with Italian terms (Largo, Andante, Allegro…) or with a number of beats per minute (BPM), for example quarter note = 120.
What are the main tempo markings?
From slowest to fastest: Largo and Adagio (very slow), Andante (walking pace, moderately slow), Moderato (moderate), Allegretto and Allegro (fast), and Vivace, Presto and Prestissimo (very fast).
How many BPM is Allegro?
Allegro is roughly 115 to 129 beats per minute. The ranges are approximate: Andante is around 76-89 BPM, Moderato 90-104 and Presto 168 to 199 BPM.
Is Allegretto faster or slower than Allegro?
Slower. Allegretto is the diminutive of Allegro, a "little allegro": about 105-114 BPM, compared with 115-129 for Allegro. It is a light, easygoing tempo, a little less lively than Allegro.
What speed (BPM) is Andante?
Andante is around 76-89 BPM. The word means "walking" (from the Italian andare, to go): a moderately slow tempo at the pace of an easy step, neither fast nor slow.
What do ritardando and accelerando mean?
They are gradual tempo changes. Ritardando (rit.) or rallentando (rall.) mean slowing down little by little, and accelerando (accel.) means speeding up. After them, a tempo means returning to the original speed.
