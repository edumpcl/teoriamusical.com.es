Minor Seventh Chord

The minor seventh chord (symbol Cm7) is a four-note chord: a minor triad with a minor 7th added on top. Its sound is dark but soft and stable (it is not a chord of tension), and it is one of the most characteristic sounds of jazz, pop and bossa nova. It is one of the five seventh chords.

How to build a minor seventh chord

Stack a minor triad (minor 3rd + perfect 5th) on the root and, above it, a minor 7th. With C as the root:

NoteChord memberInterval above the root
CRoot—
E♭ThirdMinor 3rd
GFifthPerfect 5th
B♭SeventhMinor 7th

It differs from the dominant seventh chord in the third (minor instead of major), and from the half-diminished (leading-tone seventh) chord in the fifth (perfect instead of diminished).

On which degrees does it appear?

In the major scale, the minor seventh chord is built on degrees ii, iii and vi. In C major they are Dm7 (ii), Em7 (iii) and Am7 (vi). It is also the well-known ii chord in the jazz ii–V–I progression.

A note on terminology: "minor seventh chord" is the name used with lead-sheet chord symbols and in jazz. In classical harmonic analysis this chord has no functional name of its own (unlike the dominant seventh or the leading-tone seventh): it is simply the diatonic seventh chord built on degree ii, iii or vi.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a minor seventh chord?
It is a four-note chord made of a minor triad plus a minor 7th. On C the notes are C–E♭–G–B♭. It is written Cm7 and has a dark but soft, stable sound, widely used in jazz and pop.
How do you build a minor seventh chord?
Stack a minor 3rd, a perfect 5th and a minor 7th on the root. On C: C (root), E♭ (minor 3rd), G (perfect 5th) and B♭ (minor 7th).
What is the difference between a minor seventh and a dominant seventh?
They differ in the third: the minor seventh has a minor 3rd (E♭ on C) and sounds stable; the dominant seventh has a major 3rd (E) and creates tension. The perfect 5th and the minor 7th are the same in both.
On which degrees does the minor seventh chord appear?
In the major scale, on degrees ii, iii and vi. In C major: Dm7, Em7 and Am7. It is the ii chord in the ii–V–I progression.
