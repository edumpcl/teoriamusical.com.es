Minor Triad

The minor triad (also called the minor chord) is a stable triad with a dark, melancholic or sad sound. It is built from a minor 3rd and a perfect 5th above the root. It is the counterpart of the major triad: both share the perfect 5th, but in the minor triad the 3rd is one half step (UK: semitone) lower. It is one of the four types of triad, along with the major, augmented and diminished triads.

How the minor triad is built

Start from the root. Stack a minor 3rd (3 half steps) and, on top of it, a major 3rd (4 half steps). The result is a perfect 5th between the lowest and the highest note. With C as the root:

NoteFunctionInterval above the root
CRoot—
E♭3rdminor 3rd (3 half steps)
G5thPerfect 5th (7 half steps)

Major vs. minor: only the 3rd changes

The only difference between the major and the minor triad is the 3rd. The root and the perfect 5th are the same. What changes the character is whether the 3rd is four half steps above the root (major, bright) or three (minor, dark). So lowering the 3rd by a half step turns a major triad into a minor one:

That 3rd also decides whether a melody sounds major or minor. You can train your ear with the listening exercise Major or minor?

Chord symbol for the minor triad

In lead-sheet chord symbols, a minor chord is written with the letter of its root followed by a lowercase "m". A minus sign ("−") is also used:

ChordSymbol
C minorCm (or C−)
A minorAm
E minorEm
D minorDm

Where does it appear in the scale?

In the major scale, minor triads are built on scale degrees ii, iii and vi. In C major they are D minor (ii), E minor (iii) and A minor (vi). In the natural minor scale, minor triads appear on the tonic (i), the subdominant (iv) and the dominant (v). These chords give a minor key its characteristic color. To see how each degree is named, read about scale degrees.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a minor triad?
A minor triad is a three-note chord made of a minor 3rd and a perfect 5th above the root. On C the notes are C–E♭–G. It is a stable chord, but it sounds darker, more melancholic or sadder than the major triad.
How do you build a minor triad?
Stack a minor 3rd (3 half steps) on the root and a major 3rd (4 half steps) on top of it. This gives a perfect 5th between the lowest and the highest note. On C: C to E♭ is a minor 3rd, E♭ to G is a major 3rd, and C to G is a perfect 5th.
What is the difference between a major and a minor chord?
Only the 3rd. A major chord has a major 3rd and sounds bright; a minor chord has a minor 3rd (a half step lower) and sounds dark. The perfect 5th is the same in both. That is why lowering the 3rd by a half step turns a major chord into a minor one.
How is a minor chord written in chord symbols?
With the letter of the root followed by a lowercase "m": Cm for C minor, Am for A minor. A minus sign is also used (C−).
