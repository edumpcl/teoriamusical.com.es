Triads

Triads are three-note chords built from stacked thirds. This page presents the four types in root position, their inversions and their figured-bass numerals. To review intervals first, see the page on musical intervals.

The four types in root position come first, followed by the inversions and the figured bass.

What is root position?

Root position is the arrangement in which the lowest note (the bass) is the root, with the third and the fifth above it. When the chord is not in this arrangement, it is in one of the inversions explained below.

The four types of triad in root position

Depending on the distances between the notes, a triad can be:

Diminished (diminished 5th)

From the root: minor 3rd + diminished 5th. → Complete guide to the diminished triad.

Adding a seventh to this diminished triad gives two fundamental four-note chords of the repertoire: the half-diminished (leading-tone) seventh chord (with a minor seventh, belonging to the major mode) and the diminished seventh chord (with a diminished seventh, belonging to the harmonic minor mode).

Minor (perfect minor)

From the root: minor 3rd + perfect 5th. → Complete guide to the minor triad.

Major (perfect major)

From the root: major 3rd + perfect 5th. → Complete guide to the major triad.

Augmented (augmented 5th)

From the root: major 3rd + augmented 5th. → Complete guide to the augmented triad.

The 7 triads of the major scale

If you build a triad on each scale degree of the major scale, using only the notes of that scale, you always get the same pattern of types. In C major:

DegreeNumeralNotes in C majorTriad type
I — TonicIC–E–GMajor
II — SupertoniciiD–F–AMinor
III — MediantiiiE–G–BMinor
IV — SubdominantIVF–A–CMajor
V — DominantVG–B–DMajor
VI — SubmediantviA–C–EMinor
VII — Leading tone (UK: leading note)vii°B–D–FDiminished

The pattern is major – minor – minor – major – major – minor – diminished in every major scale. In G major the chords are G–Am–Bm–C–D–Em–F♯°; in D major, D–Em–F♯m–G–A–Bm–C♯°, and so on.

The 7 triads of the natural minor scale

In the natural minor scale the pattern changes. In A minor:

II — Supertonicii°B–D–FDiminished
III — MediantIIIC–E–GMajor
IV — SubdominantivD–F–AMinor

VII — SubtonicVIIG–B–DMajor

In the harmonic minor scale the seventh degree is raised by a half step (G♯ in A minor), which turns the V chord into a major triad (E–G♯–B) and strengthens the V–i cadence.

Chord inversions

First inversion

From the bass: a 3rd up to the middle note and a 6th up to the top note. The third of the chord is in the bass.

To practice building them in a game, try Billiards: each ball carries a chord (in root position or in an inversion) and, to pocket it, you write its notes on the staff. If you would rather recognize chords, there is Bowling.

Second inversion

From the bass: a 4th up to the middle note and a 6th up to the top note. The fifth of the chord is in the bass.

Figured bass for triads

Figured bass is the shorthand of numerals written under the bass note that tells you which chord to play above it. For triads only three cases matter:

Root position

A triad in root position has no figure: the bass note alone implies the third and the fifth above it.

First inversion: 6

The numeral 6 (short for 6/3) shows that the third of the chord is in the bass: the upper notes are a sixth and a third above it.

Second inversion: 6/4

The numerals 6/4 show that the fifth is in the bass: the upper notes are a sixth and a fourth above it.

An accidental written next to a figure, or alone under the bass note, changes the note that figure refers to; an accidental alone refers to the third. Seventh chords add the figures 7, 6/5, 4/3 and 4/2.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a triad?
A triad is a three-note chord made of two stacked thirds: a root, its third and its fifth. On C the triad is C-E-G. It is the most basic type of chord in tonal harmony.
How many types of triads are there?
There are four, according to their intervals: major (perfect 5th and major 3rd), minor (perfect 5th and minor 3rd), augmented (augmented 5th and major 3rd) and diminished (diminished 5th and minor 3rd).
What is root position?
Root position is the arrangement in which the root of the chord is the lowest note. The other two notes, the third and the fifth, lie above it.
What is the first inversion of a triad?
The first inversion is when the third of the chord is in the bass. The distances above that bass note are a sixth and a third, and the chord is figured with a 6.
How is the second inversion of a triad formed?
The second inversion puts the fifth of the chord in the bass. The distances above that bass note are a sixth and a fourth, and it is figured 6/4. In C major the second inversion is G–C–E (G in the bass).
What is the difference between a major and a minor triad?
Only the third: a major triad uses a major third (4 half steps) and a minor triad a minor third (3 half steps). The fifth is perfect in both. So C major is C–E–G and C minor is C–E♭–G.
