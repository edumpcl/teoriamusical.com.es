Music Dictionary

The language of music is universal, but to master it and understand how sounds interact, you need to know its foundations. The Music Dictionary is the starting point for students, musicians and curious people who want to learn music theory from the most basic ideas up.

Recommended learning path

To study from scratch, follow this order. Each concept has its own guide, with staff examples you can listen to.

StepConceptWhy it matters
1Staff and clefsWithout reading the staff, you cannot move forward in any other area.
2Musical notesThe name and position of the 7 notes and their accidentals.
3Time signaturesHow time and rhythm are organized in music.
4Whole steps and half stepsThe basic units of distance between notes.

6Major scales and minor scalesThe 7 degrees of a key and their key signatures.
7TriadsThe four types of three-note chord and their inversions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a music dictionary?
A music dictionary is a collection of music theory terms and concepts with definitions and explanations. It covers ideas such as intervals, chords, scales, keys, time signatures, clefs and scale degrees, with practical staff examples to make them easier to understand.
In what order should the basics of music theory be learned?
The recommended order is: first the staff and clefs (so you can read notes), then the names and pitch of the notes, then time signatures (to understand rhythm), intervals (the distance between notes), keys and scales, and finally chords and their harmonic functions.
Why study music theory?
Studying music theory lets you read scores, understand how songs are built, talk with other musicians in a common language, improvise with purpose and prepare for conservatory exams. It is the foundation for any instrument and for composing.
How many concepts does this music dictionary include?
The dictionary covers dozens of music theory concepts, from the staff, note names, clefs, time signatures, intervals, chords and keys to cadences, harmonic functions and modulation. Each page includes definitions, tables and examples with staff images.
Can you study music theory without playing an instrument?
Yes. Music theory can be studied purely as theory. However, practicing it on an instrument speeds up understanding: hearing the concepts, such as an interval of a third or a major chord, fixes the knowledge much better than just reading about it.
