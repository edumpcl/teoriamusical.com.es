Articulation in Music

Articulation is the way each note is attacked, joined or separated. While dynamics show how loud and tempo shows how fast, articulation shows how each note is played: whether notes are linked smoothly or released short, whether they stand out or are sustained. It is one of the performer's main expressive tools.

The main articulations

ArticulationSymbolEffect
Legato (connected)slur (curved line)Notes joined, with no gap
Staccato (detached)small dot above or below the noteShort, separated notes

Above or below the note? Articulation signs (dots, accents, etc.) go on the side opposite the stem: if the stem points up, the sign sits below the notehead. If the stem points down, it sits above. In the examples below the notes have stems pointing up, so the articulations appear under the noteheads.

Legato (connected)

The notes are played joined and without interruption, moving smoothly from one to the next. It is shown with a slur, a curved line over the group of notes. It is the opposite of staccato.

Staccato (detached)

The notes are played short and separated, released before their full value (roughly halfway). It gives a light, bouncy character. It is shown with a dot above or below the notehead.

Accent

The note is attacked with more force than the notes around it, standing out without changing the overall dynamic level. It is written with an angle-shaped sign (>) above or below the note.

Tenuto

It means hold the note for its full value, giving it some weight or emphasis, without shortening it or accenting it sharply. It is written with a small horizontal line above or below the note.

Marcato

An accent that is stronger and more energetic than the normal one: the note is attacked in a very marked way. It is written with a vertical wedge-shaped sign (^) next to the note.

There are also combinations, such as staccatissimo (even shorter, with a wedge instead of a dot) and portato or mezzo-staccato (dots under a slur: notes slightly separated but smooth; not to be confused with portamento, which slides the pitch between two notes). Articulation is always combined with dynamics and tempo to bring life and expression to a performance.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is articulation in music?
Articulation is the way each note is attacked, joined or separated: it shows how a note is played, not how long it lasts or how loud it is. It ranges from legato (joined notes) to staccato (short, separated notes), and includes accents and other shades of attack.
What is the difference between legato and staccato?
Legato joins the notes without interruption, smoothly and continuously, and is shown with a slur. Staccato separates and shortens them, giving a light, bouncy character, and is shown with a small dot above or below the note.
What does the dot above a note mean?
A dot above or below the notehead means staccato: shorten the note and play it brief and detached from the next one, usually about half its written value.
What is the accent (>) in music?
The accent, written as an angle-shaped sign (>) above or below the note, means that note should be attacked with more force than the notes around it, standing out without changing the overall dynamic level.
