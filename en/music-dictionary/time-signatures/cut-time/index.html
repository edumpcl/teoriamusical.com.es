Cut Time (Alla Breve, 2/2)

Cut time, also called alla breve, is a simple meter with two half-note (UK: minim) beats per measure (UK: bar), equal to 2/2. It is written with the symbol ¢ (a C with a vertical line through it), and it means counting "in two" what would otherwise be written in four beats. In Spanish it is called compás partido, "split time."

What is cut time?

Cut time is the same as the time signature 2/2: two beats per measure, each worth a half note. The name comes from the symbol: common time (the C that stands for 4/4) is "cut" by a vertical line, which halves the number of beats: instead of four quarter-note beats you count two half-note beats.

How to count cut time

Count "1 – 2", leaning on the 1. Each half note divides into two quarter notes (UK: crotchets), so the four quarter notes of the measure group 2+2. The point of alla breve is exactly this: although there are four quarter notes, you feel two pulses, not four, which lightens the music at fast tempos.

Cut time (¢) compared with common time (C)

This is the most frequent confusion between the two symbols inherited from old notation:

SymbolMeansBeatsBeat unit
C (common time)4/44Quarter note
¢ (cut time)2/22Half note

Both meters hold the same total length (one whole note), but common time is felt in four and cut time in two. You can see both symbols in context in the time signature analyzer.

Where is cut time used?

  • Fast marches: alla breve gives the brisk pulse of a lively march.
  • Early and Baroque music: common in fast movements and fugues.
  • Overtures and finales: counting in two conveys lightness and speed.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is cut time?
Cut time, or alla breve, is a simple meter with two half-note beats per measure, equal to 2/2. It is written with the symbol ¢ (a C with a vertical line through it) and means counting in two what would otherwise be written in four.
What does the ¢ symbol mean?
The symbol ¢ means cut time (alla breve), that is, 2/2. It is common time (C, which is 4/4) "cut" in half: instead of four quarter-note beats you count two half-note beats.
What is the difference between cut time and common time?
Common time (C) is 4/4: four quarter-note beats. Cut time (¢) is 2/2: two half-note beats. Both add up to a whole note, but common time is felt in four and cut time in two.
Why is cut time used?
It makes fast music feel agile: counting two half-note beats instead of four quarter-note beats lets the music flow more lightly. It is common in fast marches, Baroque music and overtures.
