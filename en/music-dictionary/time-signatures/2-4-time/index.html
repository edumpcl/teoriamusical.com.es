2/4 Time Signature

2/4 time is a simple meter with two beats: each measure (UK: bar) holds two quarter notes (UK: crotchets), and each beat divides into two equal parts (duple subdivision). It is one of the most basic meters and the meter of the march, the polka and the pasodoble.

What does 2/4 mean?

In the time signature, the top number 2 says the measure has two beats, and the bottom number 4 says that the note value that gets one beat is the quarter note (one fourth of a whole note). Because 2/4 is a simple meter, each of the two beats divides in two. The strong accent falls on beat 1 and the weak one on beat 2 (strong–weak).

How to count 2/4

Count simply "1 – 2", leaning on the 1. Each beat can be divided into two eighth notes (UK: quavers), which gives the familiar "one-two, one-two" feel of a march:

Where is 2/4 used?

  • Marches: the "left-right" duple pulse of marching is 2/4 by nature.
  • Polka: a fast, bouncy Central European dance.
  • Pasodoble: the typical meter of Spanish bullfighting and band music.
  • Folk and popular music: many traditional songs and dance styles.

2/4 compared with 6/8 and 4/4

2/4 and 6/8 are both counted in two, but 2/4 has a binary subdivision (each beat in two) and 6/8 a ternary one (each beat in three). That is why 6/8 sounds rocking and 2/4 sounds square and marked. Compared with 4/4, 2/4 is lighter, with only two beats instead of four, which gives it its continuous "oom-pah" character.

To learn to tell them apart by ear, try the listening exercise recognize the meter by ear: a melody plays with the first beat accented and you decide whether it is in 2/4, 4/4, 6/8 or another meter.

How to practice 2/4

Practice the duple pulse with a steady click on the online metronome with 2/4 already selected: the accent on the first beat helps you feel the "1-2" without getting lost. To identify the time signature of a score, use the time signature analyzer.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is 2/4 time?
2/4 is a simple meter with two beats per measure. Each measure holds two quarter notes and each beat divides into two eighth notes. It is the meter of the march, the polka and the pasodoble.
Is 2/4 simple or compound?
2/4 is a simple meter, because each beat divides into two equal parts (two eighth notes). Compound meters divide each beat into three.
How many beats are in a measure of 2/4?
Two beats, and the note value that gets one beat is the quarter note. The first beat is strong and the second is weak.
What is the difference between 2/4 and 4/4?
2/4 has two beats and 4/4 has four. 2/4 is lighter, with the continuous "oom-pah" feel of marches, while 4/4 allows longer phrases.
