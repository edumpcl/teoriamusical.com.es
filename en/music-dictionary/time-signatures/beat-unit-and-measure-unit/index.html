Beat Unit and Measure Unit

The two numbers at the start of a measure (UK: bar) are not a fraction. They tell you how many parts the measure has and which note value fills each one. To understand them, you need two ideas: the measure unit and the beat unit.

Measure unit

The measure unit is the note value (or sum of note values) that fills one whole measure. In 4/4 the measure unit is the whole note (UK: semibreve), because a whole note lasts exactly four beats. (In English this term is less common than in Spanish-language theory, but the idea is useful.)

Beat unit

The beat unit is the note value that represents one beat, the pulse you tap with your foot or hear on the metronome. In 4/4 the beat unit is the quarter note (UK: crotchet): the measure holds four quarter notes, one per beat.

How to read the time signature numbers

In simple meters (the most common), the two numbers are read like this:

  • The top number tells you how many beats are in each measure.
  • The bottom number tells you which note value is the beat unit: 2 = half note, 4 = quarter note, 8 = eighth note.

Subdivision

The subdivision is the number of parts each beat is divided into. This is where the two main kinds of meter differ:

  • In simple meters, each beat divides into two (a quarter note into two eighth notes). The beat unit is an undotted note.
  • In compound meters, each beat divides into three. The beat unit is a dotted note.

That is why the numbers of a compound meter such as 6/8 are read differently. The 8 does not give the beat. It gives the subdivision (the eighth note). The six eighth notes are grouped in threes, forming two beats of dotted quarter note:

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the measure unit?
The measure unit is the note value (or group of note values) whose duration exactly fills one measure. In 4/4 the measure unit is the whole note, because a whole note equals the four beats of the measure.
What is the beat unit?
The beat unit is the note value that represents a single beat, that is, the pulse. In 4/4 the beat unit is the quarter note: the measure has four quarter notes, one per beat.
How do you read the numbers of a time signature?
In simple meters, the top number is how many beats are in each measure and the bottom number is the note value of each beat (2 = half note, 4 = quarter note, 8 = eighth note). For example, 3/4 means three quarter-note beats.
What is the beat unit in a compound meter such as 6/8?
In compound meters the beat unit is a dotted note. In 6/8 the bottom number (8) gives the subdivision (the eighth note). Each beat groups three eighth notes, so the beat unit is the dotted quarter note and the measure has two beats, not six.
