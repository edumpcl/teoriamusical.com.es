3/8 Time Signature

3/8 time is a simple meter with three beats in which the note value that gets one beat is the eighth note (UK: quaver). It is the main source of confusion between simple and compound meters: because of the 8 in the bottom number, many people assume it is compound. It is not.

What does 3/8 mean?

In the time signature, the top number 3 says there are three beats and the bottom number 8 says each beat is an eighth note (one eighth of a whole note). Each beat divides into two sixteenth notes (UK: semiquavers), so 3/8 is a simple meter, exactly like 3/4 or any other three-beat meter, but with the eighth note as the beat unit instead of the quarter note.

Is 3/8 simple or compound?

It is simple. The rule is clear: a meter is compound only when its top number is 6, 9 or 12 (beats that divide into three). With a top number of 3, the meter is simple even if the bottom number is 8. Each of its three eighth notes divides into two sixteenth notes:

Where is 3/8 used?

  • Scherzos and fast movements: a quick 3/8 is felt in one, which suits agile passages.
  • Lively dances: light triple-meter dances at a fast tempo.

How to practice 3/8

Start by counting the three beats separately on the online metronome with 3/8 already selected. When that feels comfortable, raise the tempo until you feel each measure as a single impulse. To check the time signature of a score, use the time signature analyzer.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 3/8 simple or compound?
3/8 is a simple meter. What makes a meter compound is the top number (6, 9 or 12), not the bottom number. With a top number of 3, the meter is simple even if the bottom number is 8.
How many beats are in a measure of 3/8?
Three beats, and the note value that gets one beat is the eighth note. Each eighth note divides into two sixteenth notes.
What is the difference between 3/8 and 3/4?
Both have three beats with the same accent pattern, but in 3/4 the beat unit is the quarter note and in 3/8 it is the eighth note. 3/8 is used mostly in fast passages.
Why is 3/8 sometimes felt in one?
At fast tempos, the three eighth notes of 3/8 are heard as a single impulse per measure, which gives the light, spinning character typical of scherzos and lively dances.
